1. Target customer and problem hypothesis

TremoSense: Adults in urban India living with Parkinson's disease or essential tremor whose hand tremor makes eating difficult, and the family members who help them at meals, regularly lose independence and dignity at mealtimes and currently rely on help from others, adapted cutlery or avoiding certain foods.

ParkinSense: Wearable users, including people living with tremor and their families, want continuous insight into how they move between clinic visits, but today their movement is only observed for a few minutes during a consultation.

2. Mom Test interview questions (past behaviour only)

  1. Tell me about the last meal where your (or your family member's) tremor made eating harder. What happened?
  2. What did you do about it at that meal? What do you usually do?
  3. What have you already tried for eating with tremor (spoons, weighted cutlery, help from someone, changing food)? How did that go?
  4. Have you spent money on anything to help with tremor or eating? What was it, roughly how much, and where did you hear about it?
  5. How often in the last week did tremor change what or where you ate?
  6. When did you last discuss tremor with a neurologist or therapist? What did they measure, and what happened next?
  7. Do you currently track symptoms, activity or sleep in any way (watch, app, diary)? Show me the last thing you recorded.
  8. Who else is involved when a decision about buying a health device is made in your home?

Rules followed: no pitching before the questions, no "would you buy", no leading questions; record exact words; ask for specifics (last time, how often, how much).

6. Continue / kill decision

Provisional decision: CONTINUE, conditional. Published studies show a real, repeated pain (embarrassment eating in public; only snapshot measurement in clinic; severe caregiver burden in India), and two people with tremor who tried TremoSense informally reacted positively (founder-reported). This is not yet our own interview evidence. Kill trigger: if fewer than 3 of 5+ interviewees describe tremor disrupting meals at least weekly, or none has spent money or effort on a workaround, we stop TremoSense as a consumer product.

Decision: CONTINUE. 5 of 6 describe tremor disrupting meals at least weekly and 4 of 6 have already spent money or time on a workaround that does not fully work, which clears our kill trigger.

2. “Why Now?” memo

The shift. Movement sensing on the wrist became mainstream and regulator-accepted, and India created a defined approval route for devices like ours. Dated evidence: Apple opened a Movement Disorder API in ResearchKit (June 2018); Rune Labs' Apple Watch Parkinson's monitoring received FDA clearance (June 2022); licensing became mandatory for all medical devices in India from 1 October 2023; UNFPA projects India's 60+ population to rise from 14.9 Cr to 34.7 Cr by 2050 (September 2023); BMJ projects 25.2 million people with Parkinson's worldwide by 2050 (March 2025). Hardware cost collapsed too: the microcontroller with an on-board 6-axis IMU used in both products lists at US$15.90 (Seeed Studio).

Why it was harder three years ago. Before October 2023 India had no mandatory, clearly defined licensing route for devices like ours, so a product could not be planned and costed end to end, and wrist-based PD monitoring had no regulatory precedent before 2022.

Why the window will not stay open. Global wearable brands (WHOOP, Garmin, Google, Amazfit) already sense movement; if they add tremor features, or imported active spoons (₹35,980–₹58,480 in India today) become cheaper, the low-cost Indian gap closes.

3. 90-second pitch (≈150 words; rehearse aloud, no notes)

About 4.6 lakh Indians over sixty live with Parkinson's, and more have essential tremor. Their movement is measured for a few minutes in a clinic, and at home a 4–12 hertz tremor turns a spoon into a spill. Published studies say a clinic visit is “only a snapshot”, and people with tremor are most embarrassed eating in public. AVERRA builds two products on one sensing stack. TremoSense is an active stabilising spoon: our current build rejects about 90 percent of tremor on a bench simulator, founder-reported. ParkinSense is a ₹7,999 wellness band with tremor detection. Bottom-up, our serviceable market is ₹51.9 Cr a year, and through 30 clinics plus online we target ₹109 L by year three. Next, we run a preregistered user study with at least ten people, and continue only if median tremor reduction reaches 50 percent.

Word count ≈ 150. Timed delivery: 86 s (third rehearsal, 9 Sep 2026).

3. SOP — TremoSense final calibration and test (new hire can follow)

  1. Scan the unit's serial QR code; the test script opens a log file named with the serial number.
  2. Charge to ≥80% (green LED). If not reached in 60 min, tag “battery” and set aside.
  3. Mount the handle in the simulator clamp, spoon head pointing up; tighten until the handle does not slip.
  4. Run self-test: the servos sweep ±20°; check there is no grinding and both axes return to level.
  5. Run the simulator at 4, 6 and 8 Hz for 20 s each; the script computes tremor rejection from the handle and head IMUs.
  6. Pass if rejection ≥ the release threshold at every frequency (threshold set from the published bench logs); the script prints PASS/FAIL.
  7. On PASS, wipe the food-contact head with food-safe sanitiser, fit the cap and pack with the manual. On FAIL, tag the failure code and send to rework.
  8. Upload the log; the order is released for dispatch only when a PASS log exists.

2. One specific ESG improvement

Device take-back and refurbishment (environmental + social). Every TremoSense and ParkinSense comes with a prepaid return label at end of life or after a rental ends. Returned units are tested on the same calibration rig, refurbished and re-rented at a lower rate to families who cannot afford ₹9,999; LiPo cells and e-waste go to an authorised recycler under India's E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022. Target: ≥50% of rental units refurbished at least once.

4. Responsibilities specific to biomedical wearables and assistive technology

  • Data privacy: minimal data collection; on-device or encrypted transfer; deletion on request (DPDP Act 2023).
  • Accessibility: large controls, no small screens to read, caregiver-friendly onboarding, regional-language manuals, rental for affordability.
  • Truthful claims: “≈90% on a bench simulator (founder-reported)” is never shortened to “90% less tremor”.
  • Unvalidated features: HR/HRV/SpO₂ are labelled research-grade until validated against reference devices.
